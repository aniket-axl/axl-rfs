October 2026

The Data Nobody’s Selling

In August 2026, Google paid $10 million for Spirit Airlines’ internal data. Spirit isn’t a data company. It’s an airline.

Almost every company outside the data business sits on records someone would pay for: court transcripts, maintenance logs, delivery histories, old codebases, warehouses of books nobody has indexed. AI labs want this data to train models, and funds and insurers want it for an edge. Almost none of it gets sold.

Buyers aren’t the problem. Owners can’t do the work: confirm they have the right to sell, strip out personal information, find out who would buy it and for how much, and turn one messy dataset into products. So the data sits.

For AI labs, that’s starting to change. Protege and Mercor now take raw data from hospitals and enterprises and handle the sale end to end. Everywhere else, owners get advice and a marketplace listing, then do the deal alone. Data marketplaces have failed before because every deal turned out to be bespoke. What’s missing isn’t another marketplace. It’s the company that does the work.

We don’t know the best place to start. It could be a seller’s rep for funds and insurers, a buyer for the code and data that failed startups leave behind, a tool that tells an owner what its contracts let it sell, or something we haven’t thought of.

If you’re building this, we’d love to work with you.
